## Problem and relevance

Wave processes form the fine structure of the ionosphere, so the data on traveling ionospheric disturbances are contained in the residual component of the diurnal courses of the maximum usable frequency (MUF). Radio-physical methods of ground-based ionospheric sounding are used to study these geophysical effects.

## Aim and hypothesis

The aim is to study the manifestations of traveling ionospheric disturbances by oblique and NVIS sounding. No hypothesis is stated explicitly; in substance the paper tests that the periodic components of the residual MUF component are caused by traveling ionospheric disturbances.

## Materials and methods

Results of computational and full-scale experiments on determining the MUF on the Yalchik — Yoshkar-Ola and Irkutsk — Yoshkar-Ola radio paths are presented. Periodic components of the residual component of the diurnal MUF courses are extracted, and the variations of the current spectrum of this residual component are studied.

## Results

The periodic components of the residual component of the diurnal MUF courses have amplitudes of about 0.2–1 MHz; according to the theoretical results they are caused by traveling ionospheric disturbances with periods of about 1–3 h. The current spectrum reveals “sunrise–sunset” effects — significant changes in the spectral amplitudes and a widening of the band toward high frequencies during the periods of sunrise (04:00–08:00 LT) and sunset (16:00–20:00 LT) in local time.

## Authors' conclusions

No separate conclusion is stated in the text. In substance the authors show that oblique and NVIS sounding of the MUF reveals traveling ionospheric disturbances and spectral changes tied to sunrise and sunset.

## Limitations

This is a one-page abstract: the method of extracting the residual component, the order of the spectral analysis and the sounding equipment are not described. The amplitude (0.2–1 MHz) and period (1–3 h) ranges are given approximately, without saying which path each value belongs to.

## Novelty

No novelty is claimed explicitly. In substance the paper shows that the residual component of the diurnal MUF course on two paths contains periodicities interpreted as traveling ionospheric disturbances, and “sunrise–sunset” spectral changes.
