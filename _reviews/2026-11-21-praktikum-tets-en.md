## Problem and relevance

The NI ELVIS II laboratory complex is a hardware-and-software complex for laboratory work in general engineering and specialist disciplines. Combining the hardware with software written in LabVIEW makes ELVIS II a flexible measurement platform.

## Aim and hypothesis

The aim is to develop a laboratory course in electric circuit theory on the NI ELVIS II platform. No hypothesis is stated: the work is methodological.

## Materials and methods

The hardware includes a desktop workstation, a power supply, a mounting panel, a data acquisition board, a USB cable and a personal computer with the NI ELVISmx software. The software is a set of virtual instruments that act as measuring devices. The computer display shows front panels of the virtual instruments that match the appearance of the real ones; they are adjusted with virtual “control knobs” driven by the mouse or the keyboard, and the keyboard also enters parameter values directly into the digital panels. Two laboratory exercises were implemented: “Simulation of DC circuits” and “Investigating voltage dividers”.

## Results

Schemes for studying the voltage-current characteristic are presented. After the sessions the user should be able to create and edit simple DC-circuit simulation schemes with the NI ELVIS 2 CAD tools, record voltage-current characteristics with an ammeter and a voltmeter, measure the equivalent resistance of a scheme with a multimeter, and measure the characteristics of resistive voltage dividers under different loads.

## Authors' conclusions

A practicum of two laboratory exercises for the electric circuit theory course was developed on the NI ELVIS II complex.

## Limitations

No quantitative results are given: the accuracy of the virtual instruments, the number of students who took the practicum and any assessment of its effectiveness are not stated. The paper does not say how measurements on virtual instruments differ from measurements on real ones.

## Novelty

No novelty is claimed explicitly. In substance the paper offers teaching material: two laboratory exercises that move the measurement of voltage-current characteristics, equivalent resistance and voltage dividers onto the virtual instruments of the NI ELVIS II.
